# Other regions

For any country not covered by the other region files: first work out the national model with the checklist, then use the notes below where they apply. Every country-specific fact recorded in the save file needs a live source.

## Checklist for an unfamiliar country
Answer each question from an official source (ministry, national agency, university graduate school), and note the answers in the Eligibility checklist or in a target's notes:
1. **Funding model.** Are doctoral candidates employees, scholarship holders, or fee-paying students? Who pays the stipend?
2. **Tuition for international students.** Charged, waived or offset, and at what rate?
3. **Entry route.** Advertised positions, yearly programme admissions, or supervisor-first applications?
4. **Entry requirements.** Is a Master's needed? Is a research proposal or an entrance exam required?
5. **Language.** The language of instruction and of the thesis, and the certificates required.
6. **Visa or permit.** The type, and whether it allows paid work or needs proof of funds.
7. **Calendar.** When calls and scholarship rounds open and close.
8. **Where ads appear.** National job boards, university vacancy pages, research-institute sites.
9. **Grant database.** Where the national research funder lists funded projects (for hiring signals).
10. **Scholarships for internationals.** Government scholarships for international doctoral students, university research scholarships, and schemes restricted by nationality.

## Australia
- Doctoral study is a Higher Degree by Research. Research Training Program (RTP) scholarships, allocated by each university, can offset tuition and pay a stipend.
- International applicants compete for a limited share: a university may spend no more than 10% of its RTP funding in a year on scholarships for international students. [Department of Education, RTP FAQ for students](https://www.education.gov.au/research-block-grants/research-training-program/research-training-program-frequently-asked-questions-students), accessed 2026-09-17.
- Universities usually expect agreement from a prospective supervisor before an application. Scholarship rounds and dates differ by university.

## New Zealand
- International PhD students who live in New Zealand for their doctorate can pay domestic tuition fees under a government scheme. Conditions apply to time spent overseas. [University of Auckland, PhD domestic tuition fees policy](https://www.auckland.ac.nz/en/about-us/about-the-university/policy-hub/research-innovation/doctoral-study/application-candidature-registration/phd-domestic-tuition-fees-policy.html), accessed 2026-09-17.
- Universities offer their own doctoral scholarships; check each.

## Japan
- The Japanese Government (MEXT) Scholarship has two routes: an embassy recommendation, applied for at the Japanese embassy or consulate in the applicant's country of citizenship, and a university recommendation. Embassy-route research students often start as non-degree research students before the entrance examination. [Study in Japan, MEXT scholarships](https://www.studyinjapan.go.jp/en/planning/scholarships/mext-scholarships/), accessed 2026-09-17.
- Many laboratories expect contact with the professor before application.

## Elsewhere
- Many governments run scholarships for international doctoral students (for example in China, South Korea and Singapore), and many universities fund research scholarships directly. Look up the current scheme on the government or university site, and record eligibility by nationality and field.
- Many countries fund doctoral study abroad for their own citizens through a national science or education agency. When the owner's citizenship has such a scheme, record it in `scholarships`, with its conditions (return requirements, host-ranking rules, deadlines).
