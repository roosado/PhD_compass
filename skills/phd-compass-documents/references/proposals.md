# Research proposal

Asked for by self-proposed PhD applications (common in the UK and Australia), some graduate schools and many scholarships. Template: `assets/latex/proposal.tex` (for Word, use the same headings in the `assets/word/cv.md` dialect).

## Before writing
1. Read the call: length limit, required headings, whether a supervisor must endorse it, referencing style.
2. Check how open the topic is. For an advertised project, the proposal shows how the owner would approach that project. For a self-proposed one, it must sit inside the prospective supervisor's work; recommend the owner shares an outline with the supervisor before the final version.
3. Gather 5–15 sources: the group's recent papers and the key papers of the gap, each read at least at abstract level and linked.

## Structure
Follow the call's headings when it gives them. Otherwise:
1. **Title**: specific, ideally stating the system and the question.
2. **Background and gap**: what is known (cited), and the precise gap.
3. **Questions and objectives**: 2–3 questions, each with a measurable objective.
4. **Approach and methods**: per objective, the method, data or experiment, and why the host's facilities and expertise make it feasible.
5. **Work plan**: milestones per year, including the thesis.
6. **Risks and alternatives**: the main risk per objective, and the fallback.
7. **Fit**: the owner's preparation and how the project builds on the group's work.
8. **References.**

## Lines not to cross
- No preliminary results, data or collaborations the owner does not have.
- Feasibility claims about the host (equipment, datasets, partners) need a source from the host's pages.
- Length: the call's limit; without one, ask the owner, and default to two pages plus references.

Done when every section is filled from sourced facts, the length fits, and the section 5 checks in SKILL.md pass.
