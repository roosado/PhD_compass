# Letters and statements

One document answering one question for a committee: why this owner, for this project, here. Templates: `assets/latex/letter.tex` or `assets/word/letter.md`.

## Pick the genre
| Genre | Where | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Motivation or cover letter | Advertised positions (most of Europe), industrial PhDs | Letter to the PI or committee; one page unless the ad allows more |
| Statement of purpose | US and many Canadian programmes | Essay, not a letter: research experience, research interests, faculty whose work fits, goals. Follow the programme's length limit |
| Personal statement | UK programmes, some scholarships; some US programmes use it for background and motivation beyond research | Follow the university's prompt; often with a research proposal alongside |
| Scholarship motivation | Government and foundation schemes | Answer the scheme's criteria in their order, using their headings where allowed |

When the call gives prompts or criteria, the structure follows them, not the template.

## Structure of a motivation letter
1. **Opening, 2 sentences.** The position or programme applied for (with reference number) and the single strongest reason the owner fits.
2. **Why this group or project, 3–4 sentences.** One specific, linked fact about their work (a recent paper, the project's method, a facility) and the question or idea it raises for the owner. Generic praise ("world-leading") adds nothing.
3. **Why the owner, one paragraph.** Two or three episodes from Profile, each shaped as situation → what the owner did → result, chosen to match the target's needs. Name a gap and how the owner is closing it only if the committee would see it anyway.
4. **Practicalities, 1–2 sentences.** Degree completion date and earliest start; answers to eligibility points the ad raises (language level, mobility rule, availability).
5. **Close, 1 sentence.** Availability for an interview.

## Statement of purpose (programme applications)
1. The research question or area that drives the owner, anchored in an episode.
2. Research experience, most relevant first: question, what the owner did, result, what it taught.
3. What the owner wants to study in the PhD, specific enough to show direction and open enough to fit several labs.
4. Why this programme: 2–3 named faculty whose recent work fits (linked), and resources that matter.
5. Longer-term goal, in one or two sentences.

## Style
- The owner's spelling variant, first person, active voice, short paragraphs.
- Every adjective about the owner needs an episode behind it; delete the adjective when there is none.
- Cut stock phrases ("I am writing to express my interest", "passionate", "since I was a child", "unique opportunity", "I believe I would be a great fit").
- One page means one page at the template's font size and margins; shrinking the font to fit is a sign to cut.

Done when the letter answers why this project, why the owner and the practicalities with owner-confirmed facts, fits the limit, and the section 5 checks in SKILL.md pass.
