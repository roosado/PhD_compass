# Interests and priorities

Turns what the owner wants to work on into ranked areas. The ranking drives the search keywords, the fit judgement and the report's columns.

## 1. Propose areas
From the CV, the conversation and anything the owner has said, propose **3 to 6 areas**: one core area and neighbours that share methods, systems or questions with it. For each, give one line of evidence from the CV ("thesis on X", "two projects using Y"). Include one stretch area only if the evidence supports it.

Ask the owner to rank, rename, merge, add or remove, and to give:
- 1 to 3 specific questions or directions in their own words;
- anything that would make a group a poor fit (for example "no animal work", "not purely theoretical").

## 2. Record the ranking
- Give each area a short lowercase `key` (`reef`, `optical-computing`) and a label in the owner's words.
- Fill the **Ranked areas** table and `meta.areas` with `priority` 1 for the core area.
- Put the directions and the poor-fit conditions under **Specific questions or directions**.

## 3. Keywords
For each area, write 4 to 8 search terms under **Search keywords**, grouped by key. Cover:
- the synonyms different communities use for the same thing (a method's name in physics and in engineering, for example);
- the terms used in PhD ads in the target countries, including local-language terms where ads are often written in that language (German, French, Spanish, Italian, Dutch, Scandinavian languages, Japanese…);
- one or two names of flagship methods, instruments or model systems of the area.

## 4. Draft pitch
Write one paragraph of 120 words or fewer, in the first person, from Profile facts only: background, the strongest piece of evidence, the direction they want to take. Mark it "Draft". The documents add-on refines it later.

Done when the owner has confirmed the ranking, every area has keywords, and `validate` passes.
