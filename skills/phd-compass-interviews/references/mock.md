# Mock interview

Claude plays the panel. The value is in answering out loud (or in writing) under light pressure and getting precise feedback, so ask one question at a time and wait.

## Set-up
Agree with the owner, in one message:
- the panel to simulate (the PI alone, a panel of three, a programme committee) and its tone (friendly, neutral, probing);
- length: 5–8 questions, or a time box;
- feedback after each answer, or all at the end (default: a short note after each, full feedback at the end);
- the language, if not English.

Draw the questions from the prep sheet if one exists, otherwise from `references/question-bank.md` plus the target's specifics. Mix: one opener, two motivation or fit questions, two experience questions, one or two technical questions on the project, and "do you have questions for us?".

## During
- Ask one question per message, in the panel's voice. No hints unless the owner asks.
- Follow up the way a real panel would when an answer is vague ("what exactly did you do?", "what would you do if that failed?"), at most once per question.
- After each answer (if feedback is per answer), give at most three lines: what worked, the most important fix, and a sharper phrasing of one sentence taken from their own answer.

## Feedback at the end
- **Overall:** would this panel move the owner to the next stage? One line, and why.
- **Per question:** strength, the main fix, and whether the answer used a concrete episode.
- **Patterns:** recurring habits (rambling openings, no results, underselling, over-claiming, not answering the question asked).
- **Next practice:** the 2–3 questions to rehearse again, and the facts to have ready.

Never add experience the owner doesn't have to an improved answer. If the best answer needs something they lack, say so and suggest the honest version.

Record the mock round's main lessons in the interview file (folder mode) or the save file's Interviews entry (chat mode).
